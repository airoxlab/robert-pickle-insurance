Insurance questions, answered straight

The questions we get asked across the desk every week, written out the way we would actually explain them.

If your question is not here, call (580) 471-5506. Questions before a claim are always free, and we would much rather answer one now than explain a gap later.

General questions

How much does insurance cost in Claremore?

There is no honest average, and you should be wary of any website that gives you one. Premiums are assembled from your specific facts: who drives, what they drive, driving history, what your house would cost to rebuild, roof age and type, how far you are from a fire department, prior claims, and the limits and deductibles you choose.

What we can do is give you a real quote, usually the same day, with every assumption written down. That way you can lay it beside your current policy and compare identical coverage rather than comparing two numbers that were never measuring the same thing.

What does the state of Oklahoma actually require?

Liability insurance on every registered vehicle, with proof you can produce on demand. That is the legal requirement. Homeowners insurance is not required by state law, but every mortgage lender requires it, and lenders also require comprehensive and collision on a financed vehicle.

Everything else — uninsured motorist, medical payments, umbrella liability, farm coverage, life insurance — is your choice. Legal minimums are the floor of what you may carry, not a recommendation of what you should.

What is a deductible, and how do I pick one?

A deductible is the amount you pay out of pocket before the insurance pays anything on a covered claim. Raise it and the premium falls; lower it and the premium rises.

The right answer is the largest deductible you could genuinely write a check for tomorrow without it hurting. Choosing a low deductible you never intend to use costs money every year; choosing a high one you cannot afford turns a covered claim into a crisis.

Will you look at my current policy without me switching?

Yes, and there is no charge. Bring or email your declarations pages and we will read them with you: dwelling limit, liability limits, deductibles, endorsements, exclusions, and anything missing. Sometimes the conclusion is that you should keep exactly what you have, and we will say so.

How quickly can I get covered?

For most personal policies, same day — often within the hour once we have your information and payment. If you are standing at a dealership and need a binder before you can drive off, call us from the lot. Farm and commercial policies sometimes take longer because they need more detail, but a binder can frequently still be arranged quickly.

You are a mobile agency — how does that work?

We come to you. Instead of a storefront you drive to during business hours, the appointment happens at your house, your workplace or your shop, at a time you choose — evenings and weekends included. If you would rather not meet in person at all, quotes, applications and changes can be handled entirely by phone, text, email and electronic signature. In practice this means more access to your agent, not less.

Auto insurance

Is full coverage worth it on an older vehicle?

Do the arithmetic instead of following habit. If a truck is worth $3,000 and you carry a $1,000 deductible, the absolute most the physical damage coverage can pay is $2,000 — and you pay for that possibility every year. Once a vehicle is paid off and its value has fallen far enough, dropping comprehensive and collision while keeping strong liability is often the better use of the same money.

Do I need uninsured motorist coverage?

In Oklahoma, we would argue strongly yes. The state has a persistent share of drivers on the road with no coverage at all. If one of them injures you, uninsured motorist coverage on your own policy is what pays your medical bills and lost wages. It is usually one of the cheaper lines on a policy and one of the first things a rock-bottom quote removes.

My teenager just got a license. What now?

Call before they start driving, not after. New drivers raise premiums substantially and there is no getting around that, but there are levers: good student discounts, driver training credits, which vehicle they are assigned to, and whether that vehicle still needs full coverage. Handled thoughtfully, the increase can be a good deal smaller than it looks at first quote.

Am I covered driving a friend’s truck?

Generally the insurance follows the vehicle first, so the owner's policy is usually primary, with your policy potentially responding after that. Permission matters, and regular use of a vehicle you do not own can create complications. If you routinely drive someone else's vehicle, tell us — it is a two-minute conversation that prevents a genuine problem.

Home & property

Is my barn or shop covered by my homeowners policy?

Only up to the "other structures" limit, which is commonly a modest percentage of the dwelling amount, and typically not at all if the structure is used for farming or business. If your shop and its contents are worth more than that limit — and they usually are — the building needs to be listed properly or moved onto a farm and ranch policy. This is the most common gap we find on properties in this county.

Does my policy cover flooding?

No. Standard homeowners policies exclude flood, meaning water rising from outside the home — creeks, rivers, runoff. A pipe bursting inside a wall is a different thing and is usually covered. Flood coverage is written separately. If you are near the Verdigris River or any creek that has come up in living memory, ask.

How does hail damage to my roof get paid?

It depends on three things in your policy: your wind and hail deductible, which is often a percentage of the dwelling limit rather than a flat amount; whether the roof settles at replacement cost or depreciated actual cash value; and whether cosmetic damage is excluded, which is common on metal. Two policies with the same premium can produce wildly different checks after the same storm.

Can you insure a mobile home?

Yes. Manufactured homes are written on their own policy form. Eligibility and pricing depend on the year, the condition of the roof and skirting, anchoring and tie-downs, whether it sits on a permanent foundation, and the distance to a fire department. Check carefully whether attached decks, porches and carports are included — frequently they are not.

I rent. Do I need anything?

Yes. Your landlord's policy covers the building and nothing of yours. Renters insurance covers your belongings and gives you personal liability protection, which is arguably the more valuable half. It is one of the least expensive policies available.

Life, financial & business

How much life insurance do I need?

Add up the mortgage and land balances, remaining vehicle and equipment notes, realistic final expenses, the number of years of household income your family would need replaced, and anything you want set aside for the children. Subtract what you already have in coverage and savings. The number left over is the honest gap, and it is nearly always larger than a group policy at work provides.

Term or whole life?

Term buys the most coverage per dollar for a set number of years, which suits the period when a mortgage is large and children are at home. Whole life costs more but does not expire and builds cash value, which suits final expenses and leaving something behind with certainty. Many families end up with both: a large term policy for the working years and a smaller permanent policy underneath it.

What is an annuity, in plain language?

An annuity is a contract with an insurance company. You hand over a sum of money, and in exchange the company agrees to either grow it under a defined set of rules or pay it back to you as income for a set period or for the rest of your life. The appeal for people near retirement is that a fixed annuity protects principal from market losses and can convert savings into a paycheck that does not stop when the account balance would have.

The trade-offs are real and we will state them plainly: your money is generally committed for a surrender period, and guarantees depend entirely on the financial strength of the issuing company. Annuities are useful tools for some people and a poor fit for others.

I have an old 401(k) from a job I left. What should I do with it?

You generally have four options: leave it where it is, roll it into your new employer's plan, roll it into an IRA, or cash it out. Cashing out is almost always the worst of the four because of taxes and penalties. Which of the other three fits depends on the fees in each plan, the investment options, your age and what you want the money to do.

It is worth an actual conversation rather than a default. Forgotten accounts from two employers ago are extremely common, and they are frequently sitting in options that no longer match the person's age or plans.

Do I need a policy for a barn or shop on my acreage?

If you have a few acres outside Claremore with a shop, barn or equipment, a homeowners policy usually covers detached structures only up to a limited percentage of the dwelling amount, and typically excludes anything used for farming or business. Depending on what you keep and what you do out there, the fix is either a properly scheduled structure or a farm and acreage policy.

Does my personal auto policy cover my work truck?

Frequently not. Personal auto policies commonly exclude business use, and hauling for hire, making deliveries, or operating under a business name can void coverage on a claim. If the truck earns money, it usually belongs on commercial auto. Describe what you actually do with it and we will tell you which side of the line you are on.

What is an umbrella policy and do I need one?

An umbrella adds a large layer of liability — often a million dollars or more — on top of your auto, home and farm policies, for a comparatively small premium. If you own land, livestock, equipment, rental property or meaningful savings, it is often the best value on the page. Ask for a price; people are regularly surprised how little it costs.

Claims

Should I file a small claim?

Often not. Claims follow you for years and can affect your premium and even your eligibility with other carriers. If the damage is only modestly above your deductible, paying it yourself can cost less over three to five years. Call first — asking a question is not filing a claim, and we will tell you plainly which way the math points.

What should I do immediately after a loss?

Make sure everyone is safe and call emergency services if needed. Prevent further damage where you safely can — tarp the roof, shut off the water — because most policies expect reasonable steps to limit the loss. Photograph everything before cleanup begins, keep receipts for anything you spend, and call us so we can start the claim and tell you what your policy actually does.

Does my agent handle the claim, or the company?

The carrier assigns an adjuster and makes the coverage decision. Our job is to file it correctly, explain what your policy says, make sure the right coverages get considered, and stay on it with you if the process stalls. That advocacy is the practical difference between a local agent and a claim number in an app.
